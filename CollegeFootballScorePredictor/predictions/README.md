# College football score predictions

Updated Mon Oct 19, 2026 03:26 PM ET. Refreshed automatically every day of the season and several times on Saturdays; each game's prediction is locked at kickoff and graded once the final score is in. Scores are the most likely real scoreline near the expected points; ranges cover 80% of simulated outcomes for each team. College scores scatter widely, so read the ranges, not just the headline score.

Expected points blend the model with the betting line using the weight that has predicted best in recent seasons (see Current model fit). The spread and total columns show the model's own numbers next to the line, so you can see where it disagrees. Games without a line yet use the model alone.

The fair moneyline is the American odds that match the win chance exactly, with no bookmaker margin: -200 means risk $200 to win $100, +200 means risk $100 to win $200. A sportsbook's price will be a little worse on both sides because of its cut, so a book only offers value on a team when its price beats the fair line.

The model pick is the side the model's own spread favors against the current line, with the gap in points (under half a point is no edge). Treat it as an experiment. In walk-forward backtests on FBS games these picks covered 51.0% against opening lines (2016–2025) and 49.7% against closing lines (2016–2025), short of the 52.4% needed to profit at standard -110 odds, although the line moved toward the pick before kickoff 56% of the time.
